📖 Lessons Learned
Four Things I'm Taking Forward
From experience, not theory
☁️ Cloud-first is non-negotiable
Losing an SSD hurt because the work wasn't versioned. Anything local-only is at risk. Everything now lives in the fusion-kitchen playground — backed up and collaborative.
📣 Communicate before, not after
GYB in production worked — but Patrizia didn't know until she asked. Confidence in the setup is not a substitute for a one-line Slack message to your lead first.
⚖️ Match the tool to the job
Claude is powerful but costs tokens. Rovo is free and lives inside Atlassian. Routine ticket work → Rovo. Complex non-ticket tasks → Claude. Simple rule, real saving.
✍️ Own your voice
AI can draft — but every message sent should sound like me. Over-delegating comms to AI in long collaborations erodes trust. Review everything before it goes out.

💡 Ideas to Tackle Pain Points
Four Ideas Worth Pursuing
Directly tied to what could have gone better
📋 RFC approval SLA with Sec team
Connector and plugin ISSD tickets sit open for weeks — no agreed turnaround with Security. Propose a defined review window, e.g. 5 business days from SECHELP ticket creation.
📣 Pre-production check-in habit
Before any new tool, process, or script hits production — brief the lead first. Even a one-line Slack message. No exceptions regardless of confidence in the setup.
🤖 Rovo-first rule for ticket work
Use Rovo (Atlassian) for all routine ticket handling, cross-referencing, and SOP drafting. Reserve Claude for complex non-ticket work where depth matters. Formalise as a working standard.
✍️ AI draft protocol
Every AI-generated message or document gets a personal review pass before it's sent or published. AI drafts — I finalise. One extra minute, but the message stays mine.
